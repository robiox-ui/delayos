Source: 40 contact sheets (sheet_000–sheet_039, 1 fps, 3x3 grid; sheet N = seconds 9N..9N+8). Every sheet was viewed. Key UI regions were also cropped and upscaled (bicubic) from the sheets to read small text; those crops are in `scratchpad\crops_TrNPd5U\`. Text in quotes is verbatim on-screen text. "[~]" marks text that was only partly legible.

Note on the video itself: it is a marketing piece. All captions are large, centered text over blurred gradient backgrounds. The desktop footage is shown inset as a rounded "screen" card, with a small pink ALL-CAPS kicker label above a white caption line underneath it (for example "CYBERPUNK 2077" / "Cyberpunk 2077. Started from the FlowOS desktop.").

### 1a. Installer, step by step (5:04–5:28)

The installer is a single dark rounded window (about 12 px radius) centered over a pink-mountains wallpaper. It is clearly a custom live-session app, not stock Calamares.

**Progress breadcrumb** at the top of every page: "Network · Activate · Drive · You · Region · Software · Install". Done steps have a pink dot, the current step is a pink pill with a bold label, and future steps have a grey dot. The video cuts straight from Welcome to Software, so **the Network, Activate, Drive, You and Region pages are never shown**. There is a settings or gear icon at the top-right of the window.

1. **Welcome**
   - Logo, then "Welcome to FlowOS".
   - "Let's put FlowOS on your computer. You'll need your product key and an internet connection: FlowOS downloads its newest version while it installs. You can keep Windows and all your files."
   - Pink button "→ Install FlowOS".
   - Bottom-left toggle "**Advanced mode** – Show partitions and technical details". It is off. This toggle is the only hint of a partitioning UI or dual boot; no partition editor is ever shown.
2. *(not shown)* **Network**: likely Wi-Fi or Ethernet selection.
3. *(not shown)* **Activate**: product-key entry (it is a paid OS).
4. *(not shown)* **Drive**: target-disk choice. Per the Welcome text, "keep Windows" (side-by-side) is an option here.
5. *(not shown)* **You**: name, username and password. The summary later shows the user "alex".
6. *(not shown)* **Region**: locale, timezone and keyboard.
7. **Software**: "How much should FlowOS install?" / "Either way you can add or remove apps any time later."
   - (selected radio card, pink outline) "**Recommended**" with pink badge "Best for most people": "Ready to use, like Windows: photos, documents, videos, music, archives and disks open straight away. You choose your web browser, and Windows or Android app support, when you first sign in."
   - "**Minimal**" (rocket icon): "Just the essentials: the desktop, Files, Settings, Notepad and Calculator, with a lighter look (no see-through glass). Over 1 GB smaller and uses the least memory, for small or older computers."
   - Buttons "Back" and pink "→ Continue".
8. **Install / Ready to install**: "Here's what will happen. Nothing changes until you press Install now." A "Checking your choices…" spinner shows first. Checklist with pink check icons:
   - "FlowOS uses all of Disk 1 · Drive (64 GB). It's empty, so nothing is lost."
   - "Recommended FlowOS, ready to use: everyday apps and Velotic Store included."
   - "Windows and Android app support can be added when you first sign in."
   - "Your account "alex" is created."
   - Checkbox card (unchecked, lock icon): "**Encrypt this drive** – Keeps your files safe if the computer is lost or stolen. This computer has no security chip, so you type your password each time it starts." This implies TPM detection: with a TPM it auto-unlocks, without one it falls back to a passphrase.
   - Buttons "Back" and pink "✓ Install now".
9. **Progress**: circular ring with a pink arc, "10%", "Downloading FlowOS", "1 MB of 2.2 GB" (later "7 MB of 2.2 GB"). Rotating tips in a dark pill:
   - "Tip: Press Super + Space to find any app, do quick maths, or search the web."
   - "Tip: Double-click a Windows program (.exe) to run it. FlowOS takes care of the rest." [~]
   - So this is a **network installer**: it downloads about 2.2 GB of packages, Arch-style pacstrap.
10. **Done**: green circle with a check, "FlowOS is ready", "Restart your computer and remove the USB stick when the screen goes dark.", pink button "⟳ Restart now".

## 2. Visual design spec ("Fluid Glass")

**Overall language.** The OS's own name for it is "FLUID GLASS". It looks like a Windows 11 layout (centered Start menu plus taskbar) combined with a macOS top bar and Control Center. Dark by default, with translucent frosted-glass surfaces (strong background blur, low-opacity tint, thin light 1 px borders) and a single pink/crimson accent. The Minimal install turns the glass off ("no see-through glass"), so blur is a toggleable effect.

**Top bar (menu bar)**
- Full width, thin (about 24–28 px at 1080p), translucent dark with blur. It tints with the wallpaper and turns light in light mode.
- Left: small FlowOS logo (Start or app menu), then the **active app or window name** in medium weight. Seen: "Steam", "Desktop", "Velotic Store", "Krita", "Files", "Settings", "Untitled-1 @ 50% (RGB/8)".
- Right, in order: app tray icons (a Steam icon; a Bluetooth-like or star icon), then the **"Gaming" pill** (coral/salmon filled rounded pill with a gamepad glyph and white "Gaming" text). In the desktop-section footage the pill is absent: only tray, display/notification, volume and clock show. So the pill is either contextual or only present when gaming is enabled. Then Wi-Fi, volume (with a mute glyph), battery icon plus percentage ("100%"), and date/time "Tue 17 Nov 03:38" (format `ddd D MMM HH:mm`, 24 h, UK-style).
- Clicking the right cluster opens the Control Center.

**Dock / taskbar**
- Floating, centered, bottom, detached from the screen edge. Rounded-rectangle or pill (about 14–16 px radius). Translucent dark glass with blur and small icons (about 24 px).
- Order: FlowOS logo (Start), Search (magnifier), **Task View** (overview), Files (blue folder), Notepad (yellow), Task Manager, Settings (gear), Chrome, and other pinned apps; then running apps (Steam).
- Running indicator: short pink/red bar under the icon.
- Tooltips appear above icons in small dark rounded labels ("Task View", "Search", "Files", "Settings").

**Start menu**
- Centered above the dock (Windows 11 style). Rounded about 14 px, dark translucent glass tinted by the wallpaper.
- Search field at the top: "Search apps, files and the web", with a magnifier and a faint pink outline when focused.
- "Pinned" header with an "All apps ›" chip at the right.
- Pinned grid (6 columns, squircle icons): Files, Velotic Store, Notepad, Settings, Task Manager, Android apps / Terminal, Snip, Drivers, Welcome.
- "Recommended" section: "Files you open will show up here."
- Footer: avatar circle "A" with "Alex Kim", and a power glyph at the right.

**All apps view** ("Every app, in one view"): a full-screen dark launcher, similar to the macOS Launchpad or GNOME app grid, with a small search pill at the top and a 7-column grid. It contains:
- Row 1: Activity Monitor, Android apps, Calculator, Disks, Drivers, **File Roller**, Files
- Row 2: Media Player, Network Monitor, Notepad, PDF Reader, Photos, **Ping Monitor**, **Process Rules**
- Row 3: Settings, Snip, Task Manager, Terminal, Velotic Store, Welcome

**Control Center** (drops from the top-right, about 280 px wide, rounded about 16 px, glass):
- 2x2 tile area:
  - Left combined tile: "Network – Ethernet" (pink filled circle icon = on) and "Bluetooth – Not available".

## 3. Gaming-specific findings

- **Launcher: Steam.** There is no custom game launcher. Cyberpunk is "Started from the FlowOS desktop", and the dialog shown is the **standard Steam client "Starting game / LAUNCHING / Open Steam" popup**, which Steam shows when a game is launched through a `steam://rungameid/…` desktop shortcut while the main window is hidden. The top bar shows "Steam" as the active app at that moment. Captions repeat "Straight from Steam." Steam is pinned in the dock (with a running indicator) and appears in the Velotic Store "Essentials" list.
- **Proton:** no Proton version, compatibility-tool UI or Steam Play settings are visible anywhere. Inference: Steam Play / Proton (Cyberpunk is DX12, so vkd3d-proton) for Cyberpunk; CS2 and Dota 2 have native Linux builds, so they likely run natively (the video does not say).
- **FPS overlay:** only in the Cyberpunk benchmark clips. A minimal top-left readout **"FPS: 34"**: a teal "FPS:" label, a white number, no background box, no frametime graph, no CPU/GPU lines. This is not the default MangoHud layout. It is consistent with a stripped-down MangoHud config (fps only, transparent background, custom label color) or Steam's in-game FPS counter. It cannot be confirmed. The CS2 and Dota footage shows no overlay.
- **Benchmarks claimed** (the same 2019 laptop, "throttles and struggles"):
  - Cyberpunk 2077, built-in benchmark, 1080p, High preset, **FSR 2.1 Quality**: FlowOS 35 fps vs Windows 11 28 fps.
  - CS2, 1080p, average of 3 runs: 71 vs 66 fps.
  - Conditions: "FlowOS in Gaming mode, Windows 11 stock." Peak GPU temperature in CS2: FlowOS 85 °C vs Windows 11 86 °C. So they log GPU temperature: MangoHud logging, or nvidia-smi/sensors.
  - The emphasis is that gains show "when resources are limited". This points to background-load trimming, scheduler and priority tuning, and avoiding throttling, rather than raw driver differences.
- **Gaming mode:** "Built in … One click in the top bar." It is a coral **"Gaming" pill in the top bar**. No dialog, settings or OSD is shown, so the effects are undisclosed. It was on during the benchmarks. The pill appeared when Steam was running, so it may auto-engage, or it was simply toggled on before recording.
- **"Process Rules", "Ping Monitor", "Network Monitor", "Activity Monitor", "Task Manager", "Drivers"** apps in the app grid point at gamer-oriented tuning:
  - per-process priority/affinity rules (ananicy-cpp-like)
  - latency/ping monitoring
  - a GPU driver manager (NVIDIA proprietary vs nouveau/NVK; Mesa)
- **No gamescope or Big Picture session** is shown. Games run windowed/fullscreen on the normal desktop session (KWin).
- **No kernel name, CPU governor or power-profile UI** is visible anywhere. The Control Center has no power-profile tile. The battery reads 100% (likely plugged in).
- **Anti-cheat:** "Coming in the January update" for Valorant, Fortnite, GTA Online, Apex Legends, PUBG, Call of Duty: Warzone, League of Legends, Rainbow Six Siege.
  - Reality check: Valorant, Fortnite, GTA Online, LoL (Vanguard), R6 Siege and CoD Warzone block Linux at the anti-cheat or kernel level, and Apex disabled Linux support in 2024. This claim is implausible for any Proton-based distro unless they mean a VM or GPU passthrough approach. Treat it as marketing.
- **Hardware claims** to be skeptical of: "128 MB RAM minimum" (impossible for a Plasma/Qt desktop plus Steam); "Any CPU – x64 · ARM64 · x86" (x86 = Arch Linux 32, ARM64 = Arch Linux ARM, released later); "SSE4.2 / POPCNT not required" (which means **x86-64-v1 baseline**, not v2/v3 builds like CachyOS-v3); "TPM / Secure Boot not required".

## 5. Quick notes for our gaming-focused build

- The video shows **no exotic gaming stack**, only Steam on a themed KDE desktop plus a "Gaming" toggle. The performance claims rest on Gaming mode on a thermally constrained laptop.
- So the bulk of our edge should come from:
  - kernel choice: linux-zen or linux-cachyos (BORE/EEVDF tuning), sched-ext (`scx_lavd`/`scx_bpfland`)
  - ananicy-cpp
  - gamemode
  - power-profiles-daemon
  - zram
  - avoiding background services
  - KWin effects off in game
  - MangoHud for verification
- Cheap wins that mimic the "feel": top bar with active-app name, a coral "Gaming" pill applet, floating dock, centered Start menu, top-center notification toasts, Inter font, pink accent, glass blur, rounded corners, and day/night wallpaper pairs.
- The installer UX to copy is a short linear wizard with breadcrumb dots, a "Recommended / Minimal" choice, a plain-language summary page ("Nothing changes until you press Install now"), TPM-aware encryption copy, and rotating tips during install.
